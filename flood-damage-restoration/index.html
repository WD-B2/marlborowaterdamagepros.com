Flood damage cleanup is the removal of water that entered from outside: a river overtopping its banks, groundwater rising through a floor, or runoff pushing in at doors and windows. That water carries soil, bacteria and sometimes sewage, so it is not handled like a leaking pipe.

Floodwater in the house right now: what makes it different

A pipe leak starts with clean water. Floodwater does not. Rain-swollen streams, saturated yards and overloaded storm drains pick up dirt, fertilizer, fuel and bacteria on the way, and by the time the water crosses your threshold it is usually category 2 or category 3.

Category 2 water, often called gray water, can cause illness if people touch or swallow it. Category 3 water, or black water, is grossly contaminated and includes anything that came from a sewer line or a river. Flood intrusion is generally treated as category 3 until proven otherwise.

That changes the job. Porous items that took in this water, like carpet pad, insulation and low drywall, are removed rather than dried, and hard surfaces are cleaned and sanitized before drying starts.

Safety when floodwater touches power or sewage

Stay out of any flooded room where outlets, appliances, the furnace or the panel may be energized. Water conducts electricity, and standing in it near a live source can be fatal.

Cut power only if the panel is dry and you can get to it without crossing water. For any electrical or gas risk, call 911 or the utility first, then our line at (732) 352-9660.

River, groundwater and surface flooding in Marlboro

The Monmouth County Hazard Mitigation Plan annex for Marlboro Township puts about 4.9 percent of the township in the 1 percent annual chance floodplain, with another 1.2 percent in the 0.2 percent zone. It lists 7 repetitive-loss properties and 104 flood insurance policies in force through the National Flood Insurance Program.

The plan names Texas Road and Fletcher Drive as roads that flood in intense rain and have to be blocked, and Deep Run as a regulatory floodway that crosses the township. Streambank work on Pleasant Valley Road has also been done.

It describes local flooding as tied to short bursts of heavy rain, in the range of 3 to 4 inches an hour. For your address, check the FEMA flood map and ask the township Construction Division what applies, because a home outside the mapped floodplain can still flood from runoff or a high water table.

How a flood job runs from first call to dry structure

Crews arrive in protective gear. Water comes out first with pumps and extraction units, and anything unsalvageable is carried out and photographed for your records. Then we spray and wipe surfaces with an antimicrobial cleaner approved for this use.

Drying comes after cleaning. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run while moisture meters track the wood and drywall. This stage follows the same logic as any other water job, and our structural drying process page explains the readings that tell us when to stop.

Work follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration, and whether a company works to it is a fair question for anyone you call.

Flood insurance, the exclusion and the 30-day wait

The New Jersey Department of Banking and Insurance says standard homeowners policies generally exclude flood damage. Flood cover usually comes from the National Flood Insurance Program or a private flood policy.

A new NFIP policy generally has a 30-day wait before it takes effect, so buying one when the creek is already rising does not help that storm. If you do not know whether you have flood cover, find your policy and ask your insurer.

Night and weekend flood cleanup

Floods peak during storms, which means evenings, weekends and holidays. Storm-night and weekend calls reach dispatch, and flood crews go out the same day when they are available, though no arrival time is promised when roads are closed.

During heavy rain a crew may be working another address, so describe your situation clearly: where the water entered, how deep it is, and whether sewage is involved. That helps us prioritize.

What shapes a flood cleanup quote

Flood work costs more than a clean leak because of contamination. Five factors set the scope.

  • Water category: category 3 floodwater requires protective gear, disposal and sanitizing.
  • Square footage: a flooded basement is measured differently than a whole first floor.
  • Materials: carpet, drywall, insulation and flooring each have different removal rules.
  • Drying days: contaminated jobs often need more dehumidification once cleaning is done.
  • Rebuild scope: new drywall, trim and flooring come as a separate stage.
