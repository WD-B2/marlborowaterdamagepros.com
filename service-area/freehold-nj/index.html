Culverts, creek banks and the flooding Freehold Township documents

The county hazard plan puts about 5.5% of Freehold Township inside the FEMA 1% annual chance floodplain, mainly along the Manasquan and Metedeconk Rivers and their tributaries. Named waterways in that zone include McGellairds Brook, Wemrock Brook, Yellow Brook and Weamaconk Creek. The township has 69 NFIP policies, 52 total losses and 5 repetitive loss properties, none of them mitigated.

Smaller drainage failures show up in the 2020 plan too. The culverts on Plymouth Drive, Hampton Drive and Medford Boulevard carry a tributary of the Manasquan River and are rated in poor condition, and Rose Court has storm drainage trouble and stream bank erosion. The plan also notes several homes with main floors below base flood elevation.

Water that enters from a creek or a failed culvert is flood damage restoration work, usually Category 2 or 3. If you live on one of those streets, check the FEMA flood map for your address and know who to call for pumping and drying a flooded basement before the rain starts.

If water is still spreading in Freehold

Take these steps in order, and stop if anything looks unsafe.

1

Shut the main valve

Turn it clockwise where the line enters the house. If you cannot find or turn it, call the Township Utilities Department or the Borough Water and Sewer Department, depending on your address.

2

Cut power to wet areas only if safe

Use the breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water. Wet outlets or a wet panel mean stay out, then call 911 or the utility.

3

Raise valuables off the floor

Carry papers, electronics and shoes to a dry room or upper floor. In a split-level or condo, check the unit below before you leave.

4

Avoid yard and drain water

Creek overflow, a culvert backup or a sewage backup carries contamination. Keep children and pets away from it.

5

Photograph the damage

Shoot the source, the water line and every ruined item, then record a slow video of each room.

Housing age, owner-occupied homes and an older shopping district

The Township's 2013 master plan, drawing on 2010 ACS figures, puts roughly four in ten Freehold Township homes as built before 1980. That is a dated, approximate number, but it explains why we ask how old your plumbing is before guessing at a cause. About 83.2% of Township housing is owner-occupied (2019-2023), so most calls involve a homeowner deciding on their own repairs.

Commercial buildings age too. The Freehold Mall redevelopment area on US Route 9 covers 24.83 acres with about 223,000 square feet of buildings, mostly built 1963 to 1979, and Freehold Raceway Mall opened in 1990 near Route 9 and Route 537. A tenant with water coming through a ceiling or shared wall needs commercial water damage restoration that maps moisture across neighboring units instead of guessing at them.

Freehold Borough: the county seat has its own rules

The Borough was reincorporated separately from the Township on April 15, 1919. Today it runs its own Water and Sewer Department, its own Building and Construction Office at 30 Mechanic Street and its own floodplain administrator. The county plan finds no Special Flood Hazard Area and no NFIP policies inside the Borough.

Water here is more often a drainage or plumbing story than a river story. The plan records localized flooding from poor drainage, with work done at Veterans Park and scheduled for Liberty Street Park. The Borough's history page describes Victorian-style homes, and older houses often hide supply lines inside plaster walls, so we read the wall cavity with a moisture meter before anyone opens it.

Rain, wind and outages on the Freehold record

Tropical Storm Isaias in 2020 brought the Township FEMA Public Assistance, and Tropical Storm Fay that July left 1,097 Borough customers without power. Hurricane Ida in 2021 delivered about 2.5 inches of rain to the Township with no major flooding, and an October 2021 nor'easter dropped 3.68 inches in the Borough. Heavy rain and wind in December 2023 and January 2024 cut power across Monmouth County, including Freehold Township.

Those numbers say a wet basement can still happen on a day the township reports no flooding. After any outage, check the sump pump, the floor drain and the lowest wall before you go back to bed. Wind-driven rain through a roof edge or window is storm water damage cleanup work, not flood work.

Standard homeowners policies generally exclude flood, so ask your insurer about NFIP or private flood cover. Properties toward Colts Neck or Adelphia have their own pages with the local details.
