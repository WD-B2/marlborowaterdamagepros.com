If water is still spreading in Colts Neck

Work through these in order and stop the moment something feels unsafe.

1

Stop the source

On a well, close the valve after the pressure tank. On a franchise main, close the main valve where the line enters the house.

2

Power off wet areas only if safe

Switch off the pump or room breakers only if the panel is dry and easy to reach. If outlets or the panel are wet, stay out and call 911 or the utility first.

3

Lift what you can

Move rugs, books and electronics onto beds and counters. Leave heavy furniture where it stands.

4

Keep people and pets out of dirty water

If a drain or toilet backed up from a septic line, treat every puddle as sewage. Do not run more water into the system.

5

Photograph before you move things

Capture the source, the water line on the walls and each damaged item, then walk each room on video.

Wells, septic tanks and what a leak means without public mains

The county hazard plan describes Colts Neck residents as households with wells and septic systems, and the township's Fourth Round housing plan says nearly all properties use onsite septic, with a limited number of package plants. A few developments sit on franchise public water, so check your bill or ask the township which applies to your address.

That setup moves the failure points indoors. A pressure tank, a pump line or a treatment unit in the utility room can leak long before a municipal meter would show anything unusual.

If a floor drain or toilet backs up, the cause may be the tank or drain field instead of a sewer main, and the water counts as Category 3. Our sewage backup cleanup covers extraction, removal of affected materials and sanitizing, while a septic contractor or plumber handles the tank itself.

The 1983 township inventory also notes that iron is common in well water, so an orange stain on a wet wall does not name the source. Moisture meters and thermal imaging do.

The Swimming River Reservoir floodplain and the brooks that feed it

About 7.9% of Colts Neck lies in the FEMA 1% annual chance floodplain, mostly along Swimming River Reservoir and the small creeks that flow into it. The county annex counts 17.4% of developed parcels inside that zone, so the mapped risk sits on lots that have houses on them.

The 1983 township inventory names Big Brook, Yellow Brook, Hockhockson Brook, Pine Brook, Hop Brook and Willow Brook. The township's Big Brook Preserve entrance on Hillsdale Road shares that drainage name, so a property near it is worth checking on the FEMA flood map. The 2026 annex reports no significant flood events in the past five years, yet it also records 26 NFIP policies, 41 total losses and 2 repetitive loss properties.

Water that arrives from outside after heavy rain is flood damage restoration work, usually Category 2 or 3. A wet lower level adds basement water cleanup and drying to the job.

Outages, permits and the flood policy question

Households on wells lose water pressure when the power fails, and the county annex logs 396 outages in the township during a December 2023 wind event. When power returns, a pump that restarts against a cracked line can push water into a wall or floor. Check the utility room and basement after any long outage before you leave for work.

For repairs beyond drying, the township Building Department enforces the Uniform Construction Code and is closed on Wednesdays, so save permit questions for another weekday. Standard homeowners policies generally exclude flood, according to the NJ Department of Banking and Insurance.
