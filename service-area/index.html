Marlboro Township itself

Marlboro Township is where we are based, and it is home to about 41,500 residents according to the 2020 Census. The township has 14,716 housing units, and 93.1% of them are owner-occupied, so most water damage calls here come from homeowners who need to understand both the repair and their policy.

Water comes from the Marlboro Township Water Utility, which draws about 75% from purchased surface water and the rest from township wells. If a water emergency happens off hours, the utility says to reach township police. That matters when a main line fails in the street, because it is outside the house shutoff valve.

Morganville, Robertsville, Wickatunk and the villages

The township includes several villages, and our crews work in all of them. Morganville, Robertsville and Wickatunk each have a page of their own in this section, and the same goes for the area around the Marlboro High School and Union Hill Recreation Complex.

Village streets with long driveways can change how a crew carries hoses and sets up equipment, so mention your street layout on the phone. If you are near Big Brook Park or the Henry Hudson Trail, tell us that too, since a landmark helps the crew find the right street.

Flooding and storms across the township

Marlboro receives about 48.57 inches of precipitation a year at the NOAA station in Freehold and Marlboro, and the county hazard mitigation plan lists about 4.9% of the township in the 1% annual-chance floodplain. Texas Road and Fletcher Drive are named in that plan as flooding in intense rain.

If you live near Deep Run or another low area, check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your insurer about flood coverage. For water rising from outside, see our flood damage restoration page. For wind-driven rain through a roof or wall, storm water damage cleanup is the better match.
