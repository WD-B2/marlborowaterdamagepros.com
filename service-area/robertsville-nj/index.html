Robertsville is a census-designated place and unincorporated section of Marlboro Township. There is no Robertsville mail city, so addresses here may read Morganville, NJ 07751 or Marlboro, NJ 07746, depending on the street.

If water has reached your floors or walls, this page explains how to describe your location, how to document the loss, and why drying takes the time it does. The main city page for Marlboro covers the rest of the township.

Robertsville addresses that read Morganville or Marlboro

The township lists Robertsville Fire Co. #2 at 94 Route 520 and Robertsville Elementary School at 36 Menzel Lane, and both carry Morganville, NJ 07751 addresses. Other Robertsville streets are listed under Marlboro 07746. We cover both ZIPs on this page, and 07751 is also shared with the neighboring Morganville area.

Wikipedia reports that the census place was named Robertsville in 1980, renamed Morganville in 2000 and restored to Robertsville in 2010, so older records may mix the two. When you call, give a street, a cross street and one landmark.

Landmarks and edges to name on the phone

Wikipedia describes Route 9 along the west side of Robertsville and Route 18 along the east side, with Tennent Road and Route 520 crossing it. The Old Robertsville School stands at Tennent Road and Union Hill Road, and the Union Hill Recreation Complex is on Union Hill Road. A call-taker who hears you are behind the Union Hill fields can place the house faster than one who hears only a ZIP.

Building the paper trail for your insurer

Robertsville is mostly owner-occupied. The 2010 Census, as published by the state labor department, counted 3,941 housing units here, and 95.8% of occupied units were owner-occupied. For homeowners, the claim record starts at the moment of discovery, before anything is moved or thrown out.

Photograph the source, the waterline and each damaged item, and write down when you first saw water. Keep a failed part, such as a split hose, in a bag. Photos and moisture readings can support a record, coverage depends on cause and policy, and your insurer decides, so ask your insurer.

Drying timelines by material

Standing water comes out first, as described under water extraction. Drying is the slower part, and it usually takes several days, depending on what got wet and for how long. Crews check readings on each visit and stop only when the materials read dry.

How long materials tend to hold moisture and what is checked
MaterialDrying behaviorWhat crews check
Hardwood and laminateAbsorbs along the boards and may cupMoisture reading at the board and the subfloor
Drywall and baseboardWicks water upward from the floorReadings at several heights and inside the wall cavity
Carpet and padPad holds water long after the surface feels dryPad and tack strip readings
Framing and subfloorDries slowly because it is thickReadings repeated each visit until stable

Slow drying matters because mold needs moisture. If you see growth, the mold remediation page explains how testing and removal follow ANSI/IICRC S520. Health questions belong with a physician.

The notice gives no cause for the Canadian Woods closure, and Crine Road is not shown to be in Robertsville.

Which company supplies your water depends on the address. Check your water bill and ask the township if you are unsure.

Safety before cleanup

Water can reach outlets and appliances in many rooms.

  • Stay out of any room where water touches an outlet, panel or appliance.
  • Do not run breakers while standing in water. Call 911 or the utility for electrical danger.
  • Gas smell: leave and call 911 or the gas utility.
  • Sewage and contaminated water are not a DIY job. Keep children and pets out.
