Storm seasons and the brooks behind Eatontown flooding

The county plan names four waterways that drive the borough's floodplain: Turtle Mill Brook, Wampum Brook, Whale Pond Brook and Parkers Creek Branch. About 3 percent of the borough sits in the FEMA 1 percent annual chance zone and about another 3 percent in the 0.2 percent zone, on a flood map that took effect on June 20, 2018.

Flood, hurricane or tropical storm and nor'easter are all rated high risk here. The plan describes freshwater stream flooding, not tidal flooding, so look up your own address on the FEMA flood map before assuming you are in or out.

In February 2024, eight inches of rain flooded Meadow Brook Avenue and a basement there collapsed. The practical point is timing: a basement that sits full for days soaks drywall, insulation and carpet pad past saving, so the first call should come while pumps can still change the outcome.

Wampum Dam, at the lake beside Route 35 and West Street, is described as more than sixty years old with broken gates, and the plan lists gate replacement and dredging as proposed work. If you live near the lake or along a brook, ask the borough what is scheduled before the next big storm.

If water is still spreading in Eatontown

Do these in order while you wait for the crew. Stop at any step that is unsafe.

1

Stop the source

If a pipe or appliance line failed, close the main water valve where the service line enters the house. Eatontown homes on public water are in New Jersey American Water's Coastal North territory, so call the water company if the problem sits before your shutoff.

2

Power off wet areas only if safe

Flip breakers for wet rooms only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water. If the panel, an outlet or a cord is wet, stay out and call 911 or the utility.

3

Lift what you can

Move electronics, papers, shoes and small furniture to a dry upper floor or onto counters. Pull rugs off wet hardwood so dye does not stain the boards.

4

Keep people and pets out of dirty water

Brook overflow and a backed up drain can carry sewage and soil. Children and pets stay upstairs until a crew has checked the water.

5

Photograph before you move things

Take wide shots of each room and close shots of the waterline and damaged items, then ask your insurer what else it wants to see.

6

Call the 24/7 line

Dial (732) 352-9660, give your address and cross street, and say what you see. We do not promise an arrival time, but we dispatch day or night.

Services in the order Eatontown conditions suggest

Because brooks and heavy rain define Eatontown's exposure, flood damage restoration and basement water cleanup are the calls we expect most often. Floodwater from a brook or street is usually Category 2 or 3 water, which means porous materials such as carpet pad and drywall often come out instead of drying in place.

Wind-driven rain is the second pattern. When water enters through a roof, window or siding, the work is closer to storm water damage cleanup, where the first job is finding the opening. In every case, the final stage is structural drying and dehumidification, with LGR dehumidifiers and air movers running until moisture readings match dry reference materials.

Safety before cleanup in a flooded Eatontown house

Floodwater and electricity do not mix, and a finished basement hides outlets and wiring under the waterline.

  • Do not enter standing water if any outlet, appliance or the panel could be energized.
  • Cut power only if the panel is dry and safe to reach. Otherwise call 911 or your electric utility first, then call the restoration line.
  • Smell gas or hear a hiss near a water heater or furnace? Leave the house and call 911 or the gas utility.
  • Treat any water from a drain or brook as contaminated. Do not wade in it, and keep children away.

Borough offices, water and sewer: who to call in Eatontown

The Eatontown Building and Construction Department enforces the State Uniform Construction Code and handles permits and inspections. Its page says inspections are scheduled through the borough's online portal, and that permit delays are possible, so ask the borough before assuming a repair can start on a given day. The floodplain ordinance was last updated in November 2022.

Water comes from New Jersey American Water. For sewers, Two Rivers Water Reclamation Authority lists Eatontown as a customer community, and the borough owns its local sewer system. The authority's plant on the Shrewsbury River has pump station areas that flood in heavy rain, so sewer backups after storms are worth reporting to the borough as well as to us.

On insurance, the county plan counts 23 flood policies in force in the borough and one repetitive loss property. Standard homeowners policies often exclude flood, so check your policy and ask your insurer about separate flood coverage. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not.
