About Marlboro Water Damage Pros

A call-only water damage restoration service based at 184 Route 9 in Marlboro, NJ, with 24/7 dispatch at (732) 352-9660.

Marlboro Water Damage Pros is a water damage restoration and mold remediation business based at 184 Route 9, Marlboro, NJ 07746. Everything starts with a phone call to (732) 352-9660, and that line is answered around the clock, including nights, weekends and holidays.

This page explains who we are, what happens when you call, how a job is scoped and priced, which published standards we point homeowners to, and where our work stops. If you would rather read about a specific problem first, the services page sorts every water loss by its cause.

Who we are and what we do

We are a restoration business serving homes and small commercial properties in Marlboro Township and the towns around it. Our main work is water damage: removing standing water, drying wet structures, and repairing what the water touched. Mold remediation is our second service, and it nearly always follows a moisture problem that was not dried in time.

Our crews work with extraction units, air movers, LGR dehumidifiers, moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras. Those tools exist for one reason: wet building materials look dry long before they are dry, and hidden moisture is what turns a one-day leak into a mold job a few weeks later.

We operate from the Route 9 corridor, which puts us on the same roads many Marlboro homeowners use every day. Route 9, Route 79 and Route 18 connect the township to Freehold, Eatontown and the rest of Monmouth County, so one dispatch point covers a lot of ground.

How a call to our line works

The phone is the only way to reach us, and that is deliberate. Water damage moves quickly, and a conversation sorts out the situation faster than any typed description.

1

You call (732) 352-9660

A dispatcher answers at any hour. If anyone is in danger from electricity, gas or rising water, call 911 first and call us afterward.

2

You describe what you see

Where the water came from, which rooms are wet, whether it is clean, gray or sewage, and whether it has stopped.

3

We tell you what to do right away

Usually that means shutting off the water, lifting valuables and keeping people away from wet electrical areas until a crew arrives.

4

We set the visit

We offer a same-day response when available and tell you plainly what the schedule looks like, without promising an arrival time we cannot control.

How a job is scoped and priced

Before any equipment is set up, a crew member walks the affected area, checks materials with a moisture meter, and decides what needs to come out and what can be dried in place. You then receive a written scope of work and the price, explained out loud, before work starts. If something changes once walls are opened, the change is discussed with you first.

Cost depends on a handful of real factors: the category of water (clean, gray or contaminated), how many square feet are affected, which materials are involved, how many days of drying equipment are needed, and how much rebuild follows. We do not quote numbers over the phone from a description alone, because a number given blind is usually wrong in one direction or the other.

The standards we point you to

Two published industry standards shape how professional restoration is done. ANSI/IICRC S500 covers professional water damage restoration, and ANSI/IICRC S520 covers professional mold remediation. They are public documents, and they describe things like water categories, drying goals and containment for mold work.

You do not have to take our word for anything. When you compare restoration companies, ask each one directly whether its crews work to S500 and S520, and ask what that looks like on a real job. A company that cannot answer in plain terms is telling you something.

Licensed and insured, in plain words

We operate as a licensed and insured business. That wording is intentionally general, because the paperwork that matters to you is the paperwork for your job. Ask any contractor you hire, including us, to show proof of registration and insurance before work begins.

Marlboro's Construction Division handles permits in the township, and its permit page says contractors must show a New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs registration. If your repair needs a permit, the Construction Division is the right office to ask about requirements for your address.

What we do not do

Honest limits save you time. We do not do basement waterproofing or foundation sealing: restoration cleans up and dries after water gets in, and waterproofing tries to keep it out, which is a different trade. We do not repair plumbing, so a burst pipe or failed water heater still needs a licensed plumber to fix the source. We do not do roofing, so a roof leak needs a roofer once the interior is dried.

We also do not offer fire damage or smoke restoration. If your loss involves fire, we are the wrong call, and we would rather tell you that on the first call than waste your night.

Insurance, stated carefully

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a pipe that bursts or an appliance hose that fails, is often covered by a homeowners policy. Gradual leaks and long-term neglect often are not, and flood damage from rising water is usually excluded and needs separate flood coverage. Standard homeowners policies generally exclude flood, and a new National Flood Insurance Program policy generally carries a 30 day wait.

Only your insurer can tell you what your policy covers, so check your policy and ask your insurer. We document the loss with photos and moisture readings, which helps you describe it, but we do not decide or promise any claim outcome.
