A kitchen holds more water sources in one room than any other space in a house: a sink supply, a drain trap, a disposal, a dishwasher, a refrigerator line and often a pot filler. Kitchen water damage restoration is the drying, cleanup and repair of cabinets, counters, flooring and wall cavities after one of those sources fails.

The cabinet boxes are the main problem, because particleboard swells and does not recover. If the failure was a machine rather than the plumbing, the same work is described on appliance leak water damage.

Where kitchen water damage begins

The sink base is the most common starting point. A compression nut on the supply stop loosens, a trap joint seeps, or a disposal seal wears out, and water drips onto the cabinet floor where nobody looks. The cabinet floor swells first, then the toe kick, then the subfloor below it.

Other sources sit further from the sink. A refrigerator line can leak behind a wall of cabinets, a dishwasher inlet can weep under the counter, and a pot filler feeds a pipe inside the backsplash wall. Each puts water in a different cavity, so the first visit is spent finding which one it is.

A burst pipe under the sink

One call type shows up again and again. A supply line under the kitchen sink lets go while the family is out, and by the time anyone gets home the cabinet is full, the floor is flooded and water is running into the dining room. That is a pressurized failure, and the first move is closing the main, as laid out on burst pipe water damage.

After the main is closed, our crews extract standing water, pull the cabinet contents out and open the toe kick to check the cavity. The cabinet doors and shelves are laid out to dry flat, because warped doors rarely straighten once they sit stacked and wet.

Cabinet materials: save, dry or replace

Not every cabinet fails the same way. Solid wood and plywood boxes tolerate a clean-water soak and often dry in place. Particleboard and MDF absorb water like a sponge, swell at the edges and lose the screw holding strength that keeps the box square.

If the box is intact but the finish is stained, repainting or refacing may cost less than a full replacement. We list the choice and the trade-off before any cabinet is removed.

Hidden moisture behind the kitchen wall

Wet drywall behind cabinets can hide a long time. Signs include paint that bubbles above the countertop, a faint sour smell in the sink base, a soft spot in the baseboard next to the dishwasher, or a gap where the laminate countertop edge has started to lift.

A moisture meter pressed on the wall gives a reading in seconds, and a thermal imaging camera shows the damp pattern across the whole wall. When the readings stay high, the lower sections of drywall come out and the cavity dries with air movers, as described on wall and drywall water damage repair.

Flooring under the kitchen cabinets

Water that gets through the cabinet floor lands on the subfloor, usually plywood or OSB. When the finished floor runs under the cabinet boxes, the boxes sit on top of it and trap moisture beneath them. When the floor stops at the cabinet edge, water has a direct path to the subfloor.

We drill small holes in the toe kick to feed dry air under the boxes and set a dehumidifier in the room. Tile and sheet vinyl dry quickly. Hardwood and engineered planks take longer, and readings from the boards decide whether they stay.

What to do when the kitchen floods and how it dries without a gut job

A kitchen does not have to be torn out to dry. The sequence below keeps the cabinets and counters where they are whenever the materials allow.

1

Extract and empty

An extraction unit pulls standing water off the floor, and contents come out of the base cabinets so they can be photographed and inventoried.

2

Map the moisture

Moisture meters and a thermal camera mark every wet cabinet, wall section and floor zone so the drying equipment goes where it will work.

3

Open the cavities

Toe kicks come off and small holes go through the cabinet back so warm dry air can move through the voids.

4

Dry with air movers and an LGR dehumidifier

Equipment runs for several days under ANSI/IICRC S500 practice, and readings are taken each visit.

5

Decide on repair

Once the materials are dry, we list what to keep, resurface or replace, so you can compare it with your insurer's estimate.

What drives the cost of a kitchen water loss

A leak in a galley kitchen and a leak under an island with a farmhouse sink are different jobs. The factors below set the scope, and the price is explained before work starts.

  • Water category: a clean supply line is Category 1, while dishwasher or disposal drain water leans toward Category 2.
  • Affected square footage: cabinet runs, floor area and wall length each count separately.
  • Cabinet and flooring materials: particleboard and MDF often have to be replaced, while plywood and tile often can be dried.
  • Drying days and equipment: a kitchen with an island needs more air movers than a single wall of cabinets.
  • Rebuild scope: countertop removal, cabinet replacement, drywall and paint are priced apart from drying.

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while a drip left alone for months often is not. Check your policy or ask your insurer. For a real number on your kitchen, call (732) 352-9660.
