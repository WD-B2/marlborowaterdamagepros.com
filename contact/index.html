What to say when you call

A dispatcher will ask a few plain questions. Having these answers ready gets you to a plan sooner.

1

Your address and a callback number

Give the street address in Marlboro or the nearby town, so the crew knows the route, and the number you are calling from.

2

Where the water came from

A supply pipe, a washer hose, a water heater, a roof, a toilet, a sewer drain, or you are not sure. Not knowing is a fine answer.

3

What it has touched

Which floors and rooms are wet, whether ceilings are sagging, and whether water is near outlets, the electrical panel or a furnace.

4

What the water looks like

Clear, cloudy, or dark and foul smelling. Sewage and flood water are handled differently from a clean supply line leak.

5

Whether it has stopped

Tell us if you found and closed the main shutoff valve, or if water is still running or rising.

Before you pick up the phone

Your safety comes before the restoration call. If water has reached an electrical panel, outlets or appliances, or if you smell gas, stay out of the area and call 911 or the utility first. Cut power only if the panel is dry and safe to reach. Never stand in water to reach a breaker.

If sewage is involved, keep children, pets and yourself away from the affected rooms until the crew arrives.

  • Close the main water shutoff valve if you can reach it without walking through standing water.
  • Switch off power to wet areas only from a dry spot, and only if the panel is dry.
  • Lift furniture legs, rugs, electronics and papers onto dry surfaces.
  • Take photos and a short video of the damage before moving anything, since they help later with your insurer.

What not to do

A few well-meant actions make water damage worse, so avoid them while you wait for a crew.

Do not use a household vacuum on standing water, because it is not built for it and can shock you. Do not run ceiling fixtures under a bulging, dripping ceiling.

Do not pull up wet carpet or open walls in a room that may contain sewage or flood water. Do not use a heater to dry floors fast, since surface heat can hide moisture still trapped in subfloors and cavities. And do not wait until morning to call: wet drywall and wood start to fail within a day or two.
