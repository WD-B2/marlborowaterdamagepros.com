A storm that peels shingles, pushes rain under siding or drops a limb through the roof puts water inside the house at the worst possible time. Storm water damage is the wetting of attics, walls, ceilings and floors by wind and rain that breach the building envelope, as opposed to rising water that comes in from the ground.

Dispatch runs 24/7 in Marlboro, NJ. Until a crew arrives, stay out from under sagging ceilings, put buckets under drips, lift valuables off wet floors, and call the utility for any downed line before calling us. Water that dripped into the ceiling is covered further on ceiling water damage repair.

Storm water coming in right now

During the storm, your goal is to limit the spread and stay safe. After it passes, the water already inside keeps working. Wet insulation, soaked drywall and damp framing hold water for days if nothing dries them, and the first 24 to 48 hours decide how much of the structure can be kept.

Call the restoration line as soon as it is safe to do so. Tell us where the water came in, how long it rained after the opening appeared, and what is wet. After-hours calls during or just after a storm are common, and we offer same-day response when available, though we cannot promise an arrival time when roads are blocked.

Downed lines, wet outlets and damaged roofs

After storms, electricity and water often meet in unexpected places. If water is dripping near an outlet, a ceiling light or the panel, stay out of that room. Cut power only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water.

Call 911 or the utility for any electrical or gas risk, including a downed line in the yard, before calling the restoration line. A roof that has lost shingles or a limb is not a place to stand.

  • Treat every downed line as live and stay far away.
  • Do not use a generator indoors or in the garage.
  • Do not touch a ceiling fixture that is filling with water.
  • Keep children and pets out of any room with a sagging ceiling.

How wind-driven rain gets through the building envelope

A storm finds the weak points. Wind lifts shingles and pushes rain up under the edges, flashing around chimneys and skylights lets go, and window seals that held for years leak sideways. Gutters packed with leaves overflow behind the fascia, and siding with failed caulk lets rain into the wall cavity.

Garage doors, soffit vents, bath fan caps and dryer vents are other entry points that get less attention. Water entering at any of these runs down the inside of a wall or across an attic floor and shows up far from where it came in.

Storm water versus a flood

The distinction matters for dry-out and for insurance. Rain that comes through the roof or windows is storm damage. Water that rises from the ground, a stream or the yard is flood, which is handled under flood damage restoration.

Standard homeowners policies generally exclude flood, and flood coverage comes from the National Flood Insurance Program or a private flood policy. A new NFIP policy generally has a 30-day wait. Check your policy or ask your insurer which one applies to your loss.

Drying the attic, wall cavity and ceiling after a storm

We begin with a moisture meter and thermal camera, which show where water traveled. Wet insulation is removed, since it will not dry in place, and damaged drywall is cut out in clean sections. Air movers aimed along rafters and a dehumidifier sized for the attic start pulling moisture out.

If the storm water reached a lower floor, we check the finished ceilings and the stud bays behind them. A basement that took on water through a window well is a separate case, covered at basement water damage cleanup. Our crews work to ANSI/IICRC S500 and take daily readings until the framing matches a dry reference.

What has to wait for the roofer

Restoration and roof repair are two trades. A roofer or general contractor seals the opening with shingles, flashing or a tarp, and we dry what got wet. Order matters: if the roof is still open, rain will wet the cavity again.

A temporary tarp over the opening between rain events protects the dried area. Keep the receipt for any emergency repair, since your insurer may ask for it. Ask any contractor to show the New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs registration card, which the township permit page requires.

What drives the cost of storm water cleanup

Storm jobs run from one wet room to a whole attic. The factors below set the scope, and the price is explained before work starts.

  • Water category: rain through a roof is usually Category 1, but time and contact with insulation can change it.
  • Affected area: one ceiling bay or a full second floor.
  • Materials involved: insulation, drywall, plaster, subfloor and finish flooring.
  • Drying days and equipment: attics need staged air movers and a dehumidifier, and access can add setup time.
  • Rebuild scope: roofing repair, drywall and paint are separate from drying.
