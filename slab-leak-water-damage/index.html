A slab leak is a pressurized water line leaking in or beneath the concrete floor of a house, and water that reaches the surface is an emergency. We answer 24/7. If you suspect one, close the main water valve, keep the floor clear, and avoid standing on warm or wet spots near outlets.

Slab leak water damage is what the leaking line does to flooring, baseboards and walls above it, and it can start without a puddle. Concrete is porous, so water can spread sideways under the slab before it comes up through a crack or joint.

Signs of a slab leak: warm spots, hissing and a sudden water bill

People usually find a slab leak by a symptom, not a puddle. The classic signs of a slab leak are a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water when everything is off, a water bill that jumped without explanation, and a wet or musty smell in a room with no visible source.

A warm spot on the floor usually points to a hot water line leaking under the slab. The floor feels like it has a heating pad under it, especially in a hallway or bathroom. In a bathroom, other signs of a slab leak include grout that keeps going dark, a baseboard that swells, or tile that lifts at one corner.

A suddenly high water bill is the other common clue. If you shut every fixture and the meter's leak indicator still spins, water is going somewhere. A slab leak is one possible answer, along with a running toilet or a leaking irrigation line, and a plumber sorts them out.

What to do first when a slab leak is spreading

Do these in order. None require tools beyond a flashlight.

1

Run the meter test

Turn off every fixture and appliance, then check the water meter. If the dial or leak indicator moves, water is escaping.

2

Close the main

Shut the main water valve at the house. If the meter stops, the leak is on the house side.

3

Mark warm and damp spots

Place painter's tape around any warm area on the floor, and photograph it with the date.

4

Protect flooring

Lift rugs and furniture legs off wet floors. Do not push water around with towels you then lay back down.

5

Call a plumber and us

A plumber locates and repairs the line. We dry the floor, wall base and anything the water soaked.

Electrical safety near a warm or wet slab

Water under a slab can rise near outlets, appliances and the panel. Stay out of any room where water is touching these, and do not touch a wet appliance.

Cut power only if the panel is dry and out of the water's reach. Water at outlets, buzzing or a gas smell: get 911 or the utility on the line before you dial (732) 352-9660.

A hot water leak under the slab can heat the floor enough to burn bare feet, so keep children and pets off a warm spot.

Why slabs leak: the mechanism, not a statistic

Supply lines buried in or under a slab sit in contact with concrete, gravel and soil. Over years, a line can rub against its surroundings as the house settles and temperatures change, or corrode where water chemistry attacks the metal, or fail at a joint.

A hot water line expands and contracts every time it runs, and that movement wears at the same spots. That is why hot lines are the ones most often suspected when a floor feels warm.

We do not claim how often this happens in Marlboro, because we have no local count to back it. What we can say is how it behaves when it does happen: slowly, out of sight, and often for months before the first sign.

What happens under and above the floor

A plumber finds the leak, often with acoustic or thermal tools, and decides whether to repair the line, reroute it through the walls or ceiling, or break into the slab. We do not repair the pipe.

Our part starts with moisture meters and a thermal camera to map where the water traveled. Flooring is the first casualty. Wood and laminate cup or buckle as they absorb moisture from below, which our hardwood floor water damage repair page explains in detail.

Drywall takes it up from the base and wicks it higher. Because the source is below, the wet area is often larger than the stain you see.

Night and weekend slab leak emergencies

A slab leak can surface at any hour. A floor that was dry at dinner can show a wet patch at midnight, or a hissing sound can wake you.

Night and weekend calls reach dispatch, and a crew is sent that same day when one is available. No arrival time is promised. Tell the dispatcher what you found, such as a warm spot, a high bill or wet flooring, and whether the main is closed.

A plumber is also needed, and calling both lets repair and drying be timed so the same floor is not opened twice.

What a slab leak cleanup costs depends on

The pipe repair is a plumber's price. The cleanup and drying price follows these factors.

  • Water category: a fresh line break is clean supply water (category 1), but a leak that ran for months through soil may be treated differently.
  • Square footage: a single room differs from a hallway and several rooms.
  • Materials: tile, hardwood, carpet and drywall each respond differently to moisture from below.
  • Drying days: concrete releases moisture slowly, so equipment may run longer.
  • Rebuild scope: flooring, baseboards and drywall replacement is a separate stage.

Insurers often split a slab leak into two parts: the water damage to your finishes and the cost of reaching the pipe. Sudden failures are often treated more kindly than slow wear, so read your policy and ask your insurer which part applies.

Slab leaks and Marlboro homes

Houses built on a slab are a different case from those with basements or crawl spaces, and homeowners who are unsure of their foundation type can look at the lowest floor or ask the township Construction Division about property records. We do not state how many Marlboro homes sit on slabs.

Marlboro receives about 48.57 inches of precipitation a year at the NOAA Freehold-Marlboro station, and wet ground can raise groundwater near a foundation. That can make a slab leak harder to tell apart from seepage. A meter test settles it quickly.
