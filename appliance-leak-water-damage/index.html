A split washer hose, a failed dishwasher drain or a cracked refrigerator supply line can put gallons across a floor in minutes. Appliance leak water damage is the wet flooring, wall and cabinet material left after a supply or drain failure on a household appliance. Our Marlboro crews answer the line around the clock.

While you wait, close the appliance shutoff or the main valve, unplug the unit only if the floor around the outlet is dry, and lift everything off the wet floor. If the water reached the sink base, the work overlaps with kitchen water damage restoration.

Washer, dishwasher or fridge leaking right now

Appliance failures fall into two types. A supply failure means pressurized water, so a burst washer hose or a cracked ice maker line keeps pouring until someone closes a valve. A drain failure only leaks while the machine cycles, which is why a loose washer standpipe or a clogged dishwasher drain can drip for weeks before a stain shows up.

Call the restoration line the moment water is moving past the footprint of the appliance. Tell the dispatcher which machine it is, what the floor is made of, and whether water is coming through a ceiling below. Same-day response is offered when a crew is available, and the line is staffed overnight.

What to do while help is on the way

These five actions, in this order, limit how much the floor and cabinets absorb before our extraction units arrive.

1

Close the valve

Turn the shutoff behind the washer or under the sink clockwise. If it spins loose or will not stop the flow, close the main water valve where the line enters the house.

2

Stop the cycle

Press cancel on the control panel. Do not open a front-loading washer door while it is full of water.

3

Check the outlet first

Unplug the appliance only if the floor around the plug is dry. If water touches the outlet, leave the cord alone and read the safety panel below.

4

Clear the floor

Pick up rugs, laundry baskets, shoes and boxes. Set furniture legs on foil or plastic blocks so the finish does not stain.

5

Photograph, then call

Take wide and close photos of the appliance, the valve and the wet area for your insurer, then call (732) 352-9660.

Water near the plug, the panel or a gas dryer

Laundry rooms and kitchens put receptacles, breakers and sometimes gas lines within a few feet of the leak. If water is standing near an outlet, a power strip or the panel, stay out of the room. Cut power at the breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water.

Call 911 or the utility for any electrical or gas risk before calling the restoration line. Drying starts only after the power question is settled.

  • Do not touch a wet cord, plug or extension strip.
  • Do not run a household vacuum on standing water.
  • Keep children and pets out of the room until the floor is dry or covered.
  • If you smell gas near a dryer or range, leave the house and call from outside.

Where appliance water goes after it leaves the floor

Water does not stay where it lands. It crosses vinyl seams, wicks into the bottom of baseboards, and drops through gaps around the drain pipe into the subfloor or the ceiling below a second-floor laundry. Signs show up later as a musty smell, peeling vinyl corners, buckled laminate and a brown ring on the ceiling under the washer.

Moisture meters read the wood and drywall directly, and a thermal imaging camera shows cold, wet zones behind a toe kick without cutting anything open. On carpet, the pad holds water long after the surface feels dry, so carpet water damage restoration starts by lifting a corner and testing the pad and tack strip.

A washer valve break on a hardwood hallway

A common call goes like this. The washer valve gave way overnight, the hallway hardwood is already lifting at the board edges, and the homeowner wants to know if the floor can be saved. Hardwood takes on water from the seams and the underside, so cupping starts within days, and the first move is getting the surface and the cavity beneath it drying at once.

We set directional air movers, run an LGR dehumidifier, and record the moisture content of sample boards each visit until the readings flatten. Boards that dry flat can often be sanded and refinished, while boards that stay cupped come out, which is covered in hardwood floor water damage repair. Replacing too early wastes money, and waiting too long invites mold.

How the extraction and drying work runs

Extraction comes first. A portable extraction unit pulls standing water from hard floors and carpet, and a squeegee tool clears the corners. Clean supply water from a washer hose is Category 1 on arrival, but it can slip to Category 2 within a day or two, and dishwasher drain water starts closer to Category 2.

Then the drying equipment goes in. Air movers push dry air across wet surfaces, a dehumidifier pulls moisture out of the room, and small holes are drilled behind toe kicks to dry the cavity. We follow ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration, and you can ask any company whether its crews do. Clean-water jobs on hard surfaces often finish in three to five days.

What drives the cost of an appliance leak

Two leaks from the same model of washer can land in very different places, so the quote follows the scope: what got wet, how deep, and for how long. The price is explained before any work begins.

  • Water category: clean supply water costs less to handle than gray drain water from a dishwasher or washer pump.
  • Affected area: a kitchen corner and an open basement-stair laundry room differ in square footage and in how many wet materials sit side by side.
  • Flooring and cabinet materials: tile dries quickly, while hardwood, laminate and particleboard toe kicks need slower, monitored drying or replacement.
  • Drying days and equipment: the number of air movers and dehumidifiers and the days they run set most of the mitigation line.
  • Rebuild scope: baseboard, drywall and cabinet repair are separate from the drying itself.

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a hose that bursts, is often covered by a homeowners policy, while a leak that dripped for months is often treated as maintenance. Check your policy or ask your insurer. For a real number on your floor, call (732) 352-9660.
