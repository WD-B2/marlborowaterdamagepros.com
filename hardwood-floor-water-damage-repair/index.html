Hardwood floor water damage is the swelling, cupping and finish loss that happens when solid or engineered wood absorbs water from a leak, a spill or damp air below. Boards take on moisture through the edges and underside, and they swell until they push against each other and rise at the seams.

The floor is often salvageable when drying starts early. We meter every board, pull surface water with water extraction tools, and run floor drying systems before any decision to sand or replace.

Cupping, crowning and buckling: what the floor is telling you

Cupping means the edges of each board are higher than the center, creating a shallow dish shape. It happens when the underside of the board is wetter than the top, and it is the most common sign of a leak below or a slow, steady moisture source.

Crowning is the reverse, with the center higher than the edges, and it often shows up after a floor has cupped and then been sanded before it was dry. Buckling is the worst case: boards lift off the subfloor, sometimes in tents several inches high. A buckled floor usually means a large or long-lasting soaking and needs a close look at the subfloor.

Washer valve and condensate line leaks under the floor

Two quiet leaks cause a surprising share of cupped floors. A washer supply valve that weeps behind the machine feeds water into the subfloor a few drops at a time. A clogged air conditioner condensate line, or an overflowing secondary pan, can drip onto a hallway or closet floor for weeks.

Neither leak produces a puddle you notice, so the first sign is often the floor itself. A line of cupped boards that follows a wall, or a patch beside a washer or air handler, points to one of them. The source has to be fixed before drying, and our appliance leak page covers the washer, dishwasher and refrigerator side.

Wood floors versus laminate and engineered flooring

Not all floors tolerate water the same way. Solid hardwood can swell and then relax as it dries, if the soaking was short. Engineered wood has a thin wood top over layered plywood, so it handles some moisture but can delaminate. Laminate has a fiberboard core that swells permanently once soaked, and it rarely recovers.

How we dry a wet wood floor

A moisture meter reads each board, and a thermal camera finds wet areas under the surface. Surface water comes up first with a squeegee or extractor so it stops wicking into the wood. Then floor drying systems go down: mats or panels that pull moisture out through suction, paired with LGR dehumidifiers that keep the air dry.

Heat is used carefully, since drying a hardwood floor too fast can crack boards. Gradual drying with readings each day is the safer route, and our structural drying process covers the equipment in detail. Expect several days for a typical hardwood floor, and longer when the subfloor is wet too.

Sand and refinish, patch or replace: honest trade-offs

Once the floor is dry, a cupped board often flattens on its own over a few weeks. Sanding before it flattens locks the cupping in, so we wait until readings and a straightedge say it is time. A light cup usually ends with a sand and refinish. A section with split boards, black staining or lifted seams may need replacement boards, which are woven into the existing floor.

Replacing a whole floor is the expensive end, and sometimes the right call if the subfloor stayed wet for weeks or the floor buckled across the room. The floor does not need to match a calendar. It needs to match the readings.

What to do about hidden moisture under the boards

Water under a floor that stays damp supports mold growth. Dark staining along board edges, a musty odor, or boards that stay soft after drying all deserve a closer look.

If growth is present, testing and remediation follow ANSI/IICRC S520, as described on our mold remediation work, and the moisture has to be gone first. Any question about how it affects a person belongs with a physician.

What drives the cost of hardwood floor repair

Scope and price are explained before work starts, and five factors set the range:

  • Water category: clean supply water is simpler than gray water or sewage-tainted water.
  • Affected square footage and how many rooms the flooring runs through.
  • Material involved: solid wood, engineered, laminate or vinyl plank, each with a different outcome.
  • Drying days and equipment: floor panels and dehumidifiers running until board readings match the dry standard.
  • Rebuild scope: sanding, refinishing, replacement boards, or full replacement.
