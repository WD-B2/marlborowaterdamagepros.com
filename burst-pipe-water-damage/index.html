A burst pipe is an emergency because the water is under pressure and does not stop on its own. Our crews answer 24/7, but the fastest help is yours: go to the main water valve and turn it off now, then open a faucet at the lowest level to relieve pressure.

Burst pipe water damage is what follows when a pressurized supply line splits, a joint blows apart, or a fitting fails. Unlike a slow drip, it can move hundreds of gallons into walls, ceilings and floors in an afternoon.

Burst pipe flooding right now: find the main shutoff first

Supply lines in a house run at household water pressure all day. When one splits, that pressure drives water out of the break as a spray or a steady stream, and it keeps coming until the supply is closed. A drain line that clogs is a slower problem. A supply line that bursts is a race.

Most Marlboro homes on township water have the main shutoff where the line enters the house, often in the basement or utility room near the meter, or in a garage wall. The handle is either a round wheel or a lever. A quarter turn of a lever valve, or clockwise turns of the wheel, closes it.

If the valve will not turn or the break is on the street side of the meter, the problem belongs to the Marlboro Township Water Utility. Its page says off-hours water emergencies go to township police, so call them and then call us.

Steps while you wait for the crew

Work through these in order, and stop the moment anything feels unsafe.

1

Close the main valve

Turn it fully off, then open the lowest faucet in the house so remaining water drains out of the pipes.

2

Cut power only if dry

If water is dripping near the panel or outlets, leave it alone and call 911 or the utility. Never reach for a breaker across wet floor.

3

Catch what falls

Place buckets under ceiling drips. Stay out from under a ceiling that is sagging or bulging and call us.

4

Raise valuables

Pull furniture legs onto foil or blocks, and move rugs, electronics and documents upstairs.

5

Photograph the break

Take pictures of the pipe, the water path and wet rooms. Keep the broken section if you can, since your insurer may ask to see it.

Electrical safety around a pipe burst

Water that reaches a panel, an outlet or a ceiling light fixture is an electrical risk. Stay out of any room where water is touching these, and do not touch switches with wet hands.

Cut power only when the panel is dry and sits clear of the water. A ceiling fixture full of water, a buzzing sound or a gas smell means calling 911 or the utility first, then (732) 352-9660.

A smell of gas near the water heater or furnace also means leave the house and call the gas utility first.

Where the water goes after a supply line splits

Water follows gravity and framing. A pipe that bursts in an upstairs wall drips down through the cavity, into the ceiling below, and out across the floor, sometimes reaching a first floor kitchen or a finished basement ceiling in the same hour.

Insulation holds water like a sponge, and drywall wicks it upward from the base. The wall looks dry on the outside a day later while the cavity is still wet. That is why surface checks are not enough.

In kitchens, the failure often starts under the sink or behind the dishwasher, where the line is hidden. Our guide to kitchen water damage restoration covers cabinet bases, toe kicks and countertops that have to come off to dry the space behind them.

How we stop the spread and dry what got wet

We shut off or confirm the shutoff, then extract standing water with pumps and extraction units. Next a moisture meter and a thermal camera show where the water went inside walls and under floors.

Wet drywall below the water line is often cut out a few inches above the stain, so the cavity can breathe. Air movers push air across surfaces and LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture out of it, which is the core of structural drying and dehumidification.

Ask any company whether its crews follow ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for this work. We log readings at each visit so you can watch the numbers fall.

Night and weekend burst pipes

A line can fail while everyone sleeps or while the family is away, and water running for hours is a bigger job than water caught in minutes. Burst pipe calls get answered at night and on weekends, and a crew heads out the same day if one is available.

When you call, say which floor the water is on, whether the main is closed, and whether any water is reaching a basement or ceiling. After hours the dispatcher can often walk you through the shutoff over the phone.

No arrival time is promised. What you get is an honest picture of the job and the equipment we will set on arrival.

What shapes the price of a burst pipe job

Every burst pipe is different. Five things set the scope.

  • Water category: fresh supply water is category 1, but water that sat in insulation or dirt for days may be treated as category 2.
  • Affected square footage: one wall dries differently from a ceiling, wall and floor together.
  • Materials: drywall, plaster, hardwood and carpet each dry or fail on their own terms.
  • Drying days: more air movers and dehumidifiers, running longer, add to the total.
  • Rebuild scope: replacing cut drywall, paint and trim is a separate stage from drying.

A pipe that splits without warning is often a covered loss, but long running drips and neglect often are not. Your policy decides, so ask your insurer early.
