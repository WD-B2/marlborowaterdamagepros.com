A ceiling that is dripping or bulging is holding water, and drywall that has absorbed enough of it can fall without warning. Ceiling water damage repair is the controlled removal, drying and patching of ceiling drywall, plaster and insulation after water arrives from the roof, the attic, a bathroom above or an air handler.

Dispatch runs 24/7 in Marlboro, NJ. In the meantime, move people and valuables out from under the stain, switch off lights on that circuit only if the switch is dry, and set a bucket under any drip. A leak from a unit in the attic is covered further on AC leak water damage.

Ceiling dripping or sagging right now

A brown ring means the water has already soaked through and dried at least once. A bulge, a dark wet center or steady dripping means water is still collecting in the drywall, and the weight can bring the whole panel down. Saturated drywall weighs far more than dry sheets and loses its grip on the screws.

Call as soon as you see a bulge. Tell the dispatcher what room it is, what lies above it, and whether it rained recently. Same-day response is offered when available, and a bulge that appears at 2 a.m. does not wait for morning.

What to do while help is on the way

Five quick actions reduce the damage and the risk of a fall.

1

Clear the room below

Move people, pets, furniture and electronics out from under the bulge or stain. Cover large pieces with plastic.

2

Catch the drip

Set a bucket or trash can under the drip and lay a towel in it to reduce splash. Empty it before it fills.

3

Find the source if it is safe

If a bathroom, laundry or kitchen sits above the stain, close that fixture's shutoff. If you suspect a pipe, close the main water valve.

4

Stay out of the attic

Do not step between joists on wet insulation. The ceiling below cannot carry your weight.

5

Photograph and call

Take wide and close photos for your insurer, then call (732) 352-9660.

Water, light fixtures and wet insulation

Ceiling fixtures, recessed lights and fan boxes sit in the same cavity as the water. If water is dripping out of a light or a fan, stay away from it. Cut power at the breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water.

Call 911 or the utility for any electrical or gas risk before calling the restoration line. No crew opens a wet ceiling until the circuit is dead.

  • Do not turn on a light that is dripping or has water in the glass.
  • Do not poke the bulge from directly underneath it.
  • Do not use a ladder near wet wiring.
  • Keep children and pets out of the room until the ceiling is dealt with.

Collapsed ceiling and a dripping attic: how these calls go

A collapsed ceiling call usually starts the same way. A homeowner hears a thud overnight, finds wet drywall, insulation and debris on the bedroom floor, and a hole open to the attic. We treat it as a debris and moisture job. The wet material is bagged and removed, the area is dried, and the edges of the opening are cut back to sound drywall.

A dripping attic is the other common call. The caller goes up for a flashlight and finds the underside of the roof deck wet, insulation darkened and water running along a rafter to the ceiling. We find where the water enters, dry the framing and trace the stain on the ceiling to its origin.

When the water comes from the roof

Roof leaks cause a large share of ceiling stains, and they behave differently from plumbing leaks. Water enters at a flashing, a lifted shingle, a plumbing vent boot or a chimney joint, then runs along a rafter or the roof deck before it drips. The stain on the ceiling can sit several feet from the actual opening.

We start from the stain and work upward. A moisture meter and a thermal camera show how far the water has spread through the insulation. Our crews dry and remove what is wet and protect the area, but the shingles, flashing and vent boots belong to a roofer. A temporary tarp from a roofer between rain events keeps the cavity from rewetting during drying.

A leak that appears only in cold months may involve ice backing up under shingles. The Freehold-Marlboro NOAA station records an average of 106 days a year at or below 32F, so winter freeze and thaw cycles are a real factor when you track a roof leak. If it rained, take a photo of the stain and the date, then call the roofer and your insurer.

Drying and opening the ceiling cavity

Wet drywall that is intact and has not lost its paper face sometimes dries in place. Drywall that sags, flakes or stays soft is cut out in a clean rectangle, and wet insulation is bagged. The open cavity then dries with air movers and a dehumidifier, which is the same method described on structural drying and dehumidification.

If water ran down inside the wall, the repair continues into wall and drywall water damage repair. Work follows ANSI/IICRC S500, and moisture readings from the joists decide when patching and painting can begin.

What drives the cost of ceiling repair

A single stained panel and a collapsed bedroom ceiling are different jobs. The factors below set the scope, and the price is explained before work starts.

  • Water category: clean roof or supply water is Category 1, while water that has crossed attic dust or old insulation may be classed higher.
  • Affected area: one panel, one room, or a ceiling that runs through a hallway and a closet.
  • Materials involved: drywall, plaster and lath, textured finishes, and blown or batt insulation each respond differently.
  • Drying days and equipment: attic and ceiling work often needs lift access and extra air movers.
  • Rebuild scope: drywall, taping, texture matching and paint are priced separately from drying.

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while gradual leaks and neglected roofs often are not. Check your policy or ask your insurer. To get a real number on your ceiling, call (732) 352-9660.
